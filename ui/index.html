What Overcover covers, and who it is for

One risk at a time, defined in advance. No underwriting interview, no claim form.

The risk it covers

A named city's public index — rainfall or temperature — meets a trigger defined in the product on an agreed date. That single, objectively measurable event is the whole contract. If it happens, the policy pays. If it does not, it does not — and the premium is retained by the pool.

Because the trigger is a public reading rather than an assessed loss, there is nothing to argue about and no adjuster to wait for.

Who it is for

Operators whose revenue depends on a condition they do not control and cannot wait weeks to settle: growers and agricultural suppliers, event and outdoor operators, logistics and cold-chain routes, and the software agents that run them.

It is a poor fit for a loss that needs assessing after the fact — that is what a traditional policy is for.

Built for institutions, operators and autonomous agents

Capital, terms and settlement are all on the record. Read them before you commit, and verify them without trusting this page.

Capital-backed, not promised

Every payout is reserved at purchase

The moment a policy is bought, its maximum payout is reserved from the pool and can no longer be withdrawn while the policy is live. The contract re-checks reserved ≤ capital after every state change and refuses any transaction that would leave the pool promising more than it holds.

The pool's identity — capital = deposits − withdrawals + premiums + forfeited bonds − payouts — is computed on-chain and shown live below.

Programmable and immutable

Cover is a product, defined before purchase

A product fixes the city, index, trigger, premium rate, payout bounds and capacity cap. Once published it cannot change, so a buyer is never re-priced and there is no discretion at settlement. Any account may underwrite a product with a stake, and any account may provide pool capital — the contract judges the terms, not a committee.

The same catalogue is published machine-readably in products.json, so software can price and buy cover without a human in the loop.

Verifiable, not trusted

Settlement is a rule, and the result is on the record

An index reading is finalised only by a quorum of independent operators who each sign with their own key. Anyone may challenge a reading by posting a bond, and an independent resolver quorum — separate from the operators, and unable to vote twice — decides. The owner cannot set the index or resolve a dispute alone.

From buying cover to getting paid

Eight steps. Each is a real contract rule, and the actor who triggers it is named.

  1. 01

    Choose protection

    Pick an available risk product: a city, an index and a trigger level, published in advance.

    youlist_products
  2. 02

    Understand the coverage

    Read the trigger, the premium, the maximum payout, the capacity left and the observation date. These are fixed when the product is published and cannot change afterwards.

    youget_product
  3. 03

    Buy the policy

    Pay the premium the product fixes, with the exact amount attached as the deposit. The payout is reserved from the pool at that moment, so the promise is backed immediately.

    policyholderbuy_cover
  4. 04

    The risk is observed

    An attestor submits the index reading for the agreed date, signed with ed25519. The contract checks the signature, the freshness and a single-use nonce before it accepts the value.

    attestorreport_observation
  5. 05

    The reading can be challenged

    For a short window, anyone may dispute the reading by posting a bond and a counter-value. A reading under dispute cannot settle a policy.

    anyonechallenge_observation
  6. 06

    A dispute is resolved

    The contract owner decides. Upholding the reading forfeits the challenger's bond into the pool; rejecting it nullifies the reading and returns the bond. This step is a human decision — see the disclosures below.

    ownerresolve_dispute
  7. 07

    Eligibility and settlement

    Once the window has passed, the contract compares the validated reading to the trigger. If it meets the trigger the beneficiary is paid; if not, no payout is made. Either way the reservation is released, and this can happen only once.

    anyoneresolve
  8. 08

    The result is verifiable

    The payout, the released reservation and the pool identity are contract state. Every figure on this page can be reproduced from a public RPC endpoint, without trusting the page.

    anyoneget_pool · reconcile

How this differs from a traditional claim

Not that one is better in every case — they answer different questions. A traditional policy assesses a loss after the fact; Overcover settles a condition defined in advance.

A traditional claim

  • The loss is reported, then assessed by an adjuster.
  • The payout is decided after the event, at someone's discretion.
  • Payment arrives on a queue measured in weeks.
  • The policy wording is long, and its interpretation is contestable.

How Overcover settles

  • The trigger is a public reading, fixed in the product before you buy.
  • Eligibility is a comparison — reading against trigger — not a judgement call.
  • The payout is reserved from capital the pool already holds.
  • Settlement can be replayed and checked by anyone, on-chain.

1Defined conditions

The city, index, trigger, premium and payout bounds are published before purchase and cannot change afterwards. There is no re-pricing after the fact.

2Reserved payout

The moment a policy is bought, its payout is reserved. The contract refuses any transaction that would leave the pool promising more than it holds.

3Bonded, challengeable reading

The attestor's reading is signed and verified on-chain, and anyone may dispute it with a bond. A disputed reading cannot settle a policy.

What you should know before trusting this

  • Testnet only, unaudited. This runs on NEAR testnet with test funds. The contract has not been professionally audited and is not for real money.
  • Single trusted attestor. The index reading is currently signed by one key, not a decentralised oracle. It can be challenged, but the challenge is not yet trustless.
  • The owner resolves disputes. Step 06 is a human decision by the contract owner. The owner has no power to move pool capital, but it does decide disputes.
  • 60-second challenge window. The live demonstration uses a 60-second window; a production deployment would use a longer one.
  • Parametric, not indemnity. A payout follows the reading meeting the trigger. It does not compensate an assessed loss, and a loss that does not meet the trigger is not covered.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Short, direct answers to what operators, agents and reviewers ask first. Everything here is a property of the deployed contract, not a marketing claim.

What exactly does Overcover cover?

A single, publicly measurable event: a named city's rainfall or temperature reading meeting a trigger fixed in the product before you buy, on an agreed date. If the reading meets the trigger the policy pays its reserved payout; if it does not, it pays nothing and the premium is retained by the pool. It is parametric, not indemnity — it does not assess a loss after the fact.

Who is Overcover for?

Operators whose revenue depends on a condition they do not control and cannot wait weeks to settle — growers and agricultural suppliers, event and outdoor operators, logistics and cold-chain routes — and the software agents that run them. It is a poor fit for a loss that needs assessing after the fact.

How is the payout guaranteed?

The moment a policy is bought, its maximum payout is reserved from the pool and cannot be withdrawn while the policy is live. The contract re-checks reserved ≤ capital after every state change and refuses any transaction that would leave the pool promising more than it holds. The pool identity capital = deposits − withdrawals + premiums + forfeited bonds − payouts is computed on-chain and shown live.

How does an autonomous agent buy cover?

The agent has its own NEAR account and key, and a human sets hard spending limits in advance. It reads the same machine-readable catalogue (products.json) a person reads, calls the contract's own quote view, and signs a single buy_cover call — identical to the one a human wallet signs. The public console can evaluate a purchase with the agent's spending-policy engine read-only, but it holds no key and signs nothing.

Who decides the reading, and can it be disputed?

A reading is finalised only by a quorum of independent operators who each sign with their own key; no single operator and not the owner can set the value alone. For a short window anyone may dispute a reading by posting a bond and a counter-value. A quorum of independent resolvers — separate from the operators, and unable to vote twice — decides; the owner cannot resolve a dispute alone.

How is settlement verified?

Settlement is a single comparison, reading against trigger, run once when the challenge window has passed. The payout, the released reservation and the pool identity are all contract state, readable from public JSON-RPC without trusting the page. The console reproduces every figure from a public RPC endpoint, and a failed read is shown as stale or “no data” — never as live.

Which NEAR contract is current, and which is legacy?

The current, hardened contract — the default for new cover — is contract.ovc-v3-ctl-2f6c4c.testnet. The original v1 module lives at contract.attestor-overcover.testnet; it is readable for the record but is not a purchase destination, and the console labels it “Legacy build” and disables buying there. The console decides which is which by reading the deployed code hash, not from a label on the page.

Is this ready for real money?

No. Overcover runs on NEAR testnet with test funds only, and the contract has not been professionally audited. It is a working prototype: the full lifecycle — purchase, oracle quorum, bonded dispute, settlement and pool accounting — runs on-chain, but it is not for real funds yet.
